Read and write binary numbers

Binary numbers use only 0 and 1. Each digit is called a bit.

Decimal means our ordinary counting numbers, such as 6 or 13.

First, turn a decimal number into binary by dividing by 2. Then turn binary back into decimal using powers of 2.

Convert decimal 13 to binary

Change decimal 13 into binary. Divide by 2. Use the whole-number answer in the next division. Repeat until that answer is 0.

13 ÷ 2 = 6 remainder 1 means six groups of 2, with 1 left over. The whole-number answer, 6, is the quotient. The leftover 1 is the remainder.

Follow every row. Save each remainder.

Every division needed to convert decimal 13
DivisionWhat happens next?
13 ÷ 2 = 6 remainder 1Save 1. Divide 6 by 2 next.
6 ÷ 2 = 3 remainder 0Save 0. Divide 3 by 2 next.
3 ÷ 2 = 1 remainder 1Save 1. Divide 1 by 2 next.
1 ÷ 2 = 0 remainder 1Save 1. Stop because the answer is 0.

Read the remainder column from bottom to top: 1, 1, 0, 1. Put those digits together. Decimal 13 = binary 1101.

Start at the right with position 0. Move left: 1, 2, 3.

2. Work out the powers

The small raised number is called an exponent. In 23, the 3 means multiply three 2s: 2 × 2 × 2.

Start with 20 = 1. Each time you move one position left, double the value: 1, 2, 4, 8.

Match each position to its power of 2.

Optional: Say binary or decimal

The digits 1000 can mean two different numbers. Decimal 1000 is one thousand. Binary 1000 is 8.

In binary 1000, the 1 is at position 3. Its power is 23 = 8.

Multiply each digit by its power of 2. Add the results.

Binary 1000Binary digit goes before ×. Position becomes the small raised exponent.
Binary digit1000
Position3210
Power23 = 822 = 421 = 220 = 1
Multiply1 × 230 × 220 × 210 × 20
Result8000

8 + 0 + 0 + 0 = 8. Say "binary 1000" so the reader knows which rules to use.

Optional: Zeros at the start

Adding zeros at the start does not change a binary number. Binary 11 and binary 0011 both mean 3.

Each starting zero adds 0 because 0 × any number = 0.
