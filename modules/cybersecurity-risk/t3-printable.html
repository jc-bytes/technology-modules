Spot online dangers

Inspect the message

Fictional message for practice

You won a tablet. Reply with your school password to claim it.
Clue: asks for a password→Danger: phishing→Check through the teacher or official school site

The prize is a lure. The password request is the evidence: sharing it could let someone enter your account.

Inspect the second model

A drawing tool locks the work→A payment demand appears→Tell teacher/IT; recover a clean backup

Locked work plus a demand for money suggests ransomware. A separate backup helps recover work after infection.

Read the two models

Phishing is a fake message that tries to steal private information. Malware is software made to harm a computer or steal information.

“You won a tablet. Reply with your school password to claim it.” The password request reveals phishing. Show the message to the teacher and check through the official school site. This keeps the password away from the sender.

A fake drawing tool locks a student’s work and demands money. Locked files and a payment demand suggest ransomware. Tell the teacher so school IT can isolate the device and restore a clean backup. A separate backup helps recover work; it does not stop the first infection.

Repeated password guessing threatens an account. A long, unique password makes guessing harder. Multi-factor authentication adds a separate verification step.

Two threats, different protections

Ransomware

Work becomes locked→Money demanded→Ask teacher/IT; recover clean backup

A separate backup helps recovery. It does not prevent infection.

Brute force

Repeated guesses→Account access at risk→Long unique password + MFA

Repeated attempts are evidence of guessing; a second factor adds another barrier.

1 Read the examples

Phishing: A trick that uses a fake message to steal private information. Malware: Software made to harm a computer or steal information.

A threat can cause harm. A protection reduces the chance of harm. Read the clue, name the danger, choose a protection, and explain how it helps. Verify unexpected messages through the official school website or a trusted adult. Report suspicious messages to the teacher.

Ransomware: malware that locks files and demands payment. A backup is a separate saved copy that helps recover lost files. Brute-force attack: repeated password guesses. A long, unique password makes guessing harder. Multi-factor authentication adds another verification step to protect an account when a password is stolen.

Model: a fake drawing tool locks a student’s work and demands money. The clue is locked files plus a payment demand. This is ransomware. Tell the teacher so school IT can isolate the affected device and restore a clean backup. A separate backup helps recover the work; it does not stop the first infection.

Compare online risks

Probability and impact

Probability means how likely something is to happen. Impact means how much harm it could cause. Likelihood means the same as probability here. Use a different case fact for each judgement.

For P1, use low or high. For the guided discussion and later presentation, medium is also available when supported by facts. A checked recovery copy can reduce permanent loss.

Place the case on the risk grid

Practice A: School email is left signed in on a library computer. Another user is waiting. It contains classwork and private messages.

High probabilityOften, little harmOften, serious harm

Point to a different case fact for each choice. Then explain how your protection helps.

Model: an open email account

Practice A: A pupil leaves school email signed in on a library computer. Another person is waiting. The email contains classwork and private messages.

Probability is high because the account is open and another user is waiting. Impact is high because private information or work could be exposed. Sign out before leaving to remove access through that session.

A recovery copy changes the harm

Five editors; a deletion yesterday→Version history restored the file→A checked backup exists this morning

Discuss likelihood and impact using low, medium or high and a fact. The model uses medium for both: another deletion is plausible, but recovery reduces permanent loss. Another rating needs a reason that fits the case.
